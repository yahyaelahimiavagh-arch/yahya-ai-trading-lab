## 1. Executive summary

The target is **Mehmet Aydın, publicly called Tosuncuk, and Çiftlik Bank**. It is not an unrelated Mehmet Tosun. The official Turkish ministry notice identifies the domestic operating cluster and its KKTC software entity [S01]; judicial reporting identifies Mehmet and his brother Fatih [S04, S05].

The strongest evidence supports a **participant-money-dependent system**, with real businesses and assets that did not establish a sufficient independent source of participant returns. The December 2017 SPK announcement already said the companies were loss-making and the advertised payments could not be met [S02]. The ministry subsequently rejected the claimed integrated production-to-distribution explanation [S01]. The indictment describes incoming participant money supporting earlier players' apparent gains [S07]. These are distinct evidence layers: a regulator's finding, a ministry finding, and a prosecution allegation—not an independently reconstructed transaction ledger.

The principal reported official-accounting envelope is TRY 1,139,972,622 collected and TRY 687,838,995 paid. Their difference is **TRY 452,133,627**, a net cash-inflow subtotal, **not a proven theft total, remaining principal, or unexplained loss**. The public figures cannot close a consolidated cashflow statement. Asset stock, company transfers, accounting losses, foreign transfers and mining expenditures cannot be indiscriminately subtracted from one another. Section 5 supplies the bounded reconciliation.

Some farms, birds, shops and overseas assets existed. Their existence does not prove profitability, ownership free of encumbrances, or availability to Turkish participants. A documented sale of birds and the Uruguay forfeiture dispute illustrate why operating assets, realizable assets and restitution are different categories [S12, S20]. No independently verified mining wallet, pool settlement history or attributable mining profit was obtained.

The latest verified principal criminal disposition located is the **3 February 2025 first-instance judgment**, sentencing the brothers to 45,376 years and six months each [S04]. This research explicitly searched beyond that date. It did not verify a subsequent appellate disposition of that principal judgment. The reported appellate affirmance concerning unauthorized use of “bank” belongs to a separate proceeding [S06]. Legal finality remains unverified; search absence is not proof that no later proceeding exists.

The largest incremental YATL lesson is to make **the source of every equity change explicit**, then separately prove claims about performance and the coverage of obligations. Existing YATL already has useful fixed-capital accounting, cost treatment, replay and evidence-grounding controls. This report recommends a bounded extension of those controls, with third-party-capital controls conditional on a separately authorized future scope. Nothing here authorizes collecting outside capital.

## 3. User-facing business model

The reconstructed path is:

1. A participant supplied money through the available collection channels.
2. The platform credited game currency (“gold”) and allowed virtual-animal/farm purchases.
3. Software represented animal production and credited virtual proceeds after game deductions.
4. A displayed balance became a withdrawal request or in-system spending/reinvestment.
5. A payment processor/bank transfer, if actually settled, delivered cash to the participant.

Only step 5 proves a payment occurred. None of those steps alone proves a profitable real-world sale.

The indictment describes one-year virtual animals, initial referral advantages followed by dealership/clan structures, and a 95% income/5% shop-credit allocation after relevant game deductions. It describes the move from personal/company accounts to Papara [S07]. Archived terms instead describe rewards as advertising-budget distributions, distinguish gross displayed yields from costs and deny a fixed payment promise [S08]. That tension is central: the product's legal wording, marketing and funding mechanism must be evaluated separately.

| Mechanism | What can be reconstructed | Remaining boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Deposits | Personal/company transfers and electronic payment rails; Aydın also described shop cash/card collections [S07, S14] | No complete processor settlement reconciliation, channel calendar or refund ledger. |
| Withdrawals | Processor-linked cashouts; January 2018 reporting describes daily-to-monthly restrictions and payment interruption [S10] | Exact per-version minimums, fees, queues, approval discretion and final settlement dates unavailable. |
| Frequency | Daily virtual reward material is not equivalent to daily bank settlement | Contract/game versions changed; no universal payout schedule established. |
| Returns | Gross marketing yields and game deductions existed | No uniform, verified net annual return applying to every animal/cohort. |
| Referrals/rewards | Referral, dealership and clan incentives promoted recruitment and reinvestment | Exact commission ladders, bonus funding and all version dates unresolved. |
| Animal linkage | Real facilities existed alongside virtual units | No ear-tag/serial-level title, livestock register or one-to-one animal-to-member mapping obtained. |
| Production pooling | Company-level economic claims rather than verified individual animal output | A legally segregated participant production pool was not demonstrated. |
| Verification | Dashboard balances were operator-generated records | No independent reconciliation to quantity produced, third-party invoices, cash receipts and net distributable profit. |

The missing production-to-payment chain would require animal inventory → daily output → buyer invoice → bank receipt → full operating costs → available distributable cash → participant allocation. A farm visit verifies only part of the first link.

### 5.2 Required consolidated identity

For an identified period and entity boundary:

    closing cash − opening cash
      = participant receipts + external operating receipts
        + debt/equity financing + asset-sale receipts + other proven receipts
        − participant settlements − third-party capex − operating cash expenses
        − nonproductive purchases − net transfers outside the boundary
        − other proven cash uses + unresolved reconciliation difference

Internal movements cancel. Crypto acquisition is an asset conversion; rig purchases are capex; mined coins are a separate receipt; coin sales and unrealized coin appreciation are different events. Each item needs a unique transaction key and period. The user's desired participant-inflow-minus-uses bridge is a useful starting view, but other genuine receipts and cash stocks must be included before it can be an accounting reconciliation.

## 10. Return plausibility

### 10.1 Normalize the promised return before testing it

Contemporary sources report differing high-yield descriptions, including 160–307% in the press account of SPK and 180% gross/other figures in sector coverage [S09, S11]. The archived contract expressly distinguishes gross display from deductions [C08]. A 180% **total cash return including principal** means an 80% gain; a 180% **profit** means 280% total cash. Neither interpretation can be applied universally without the actual animal table and cohort contract. This report does not impose a fictional single APR.

For any product version, the required bridge is: purchase price → dated net rewards → principal repayment or expiry → all fees/credits → annualized participant return. For the productive business, use operating surplus after feed, labor, energy, land/lease, veterinary costs, mortality, logistics, taxes where applicable, maintenance, depreciation and working capital. Gross farm or shop turnover is not the available return.

### 10.2 Historically grounded scale checks

**Eggs.** The Agriculture Ministry's TEPGE report reproduces a 2017 producer price of approximately TRY 0.31 per egg [S29, appendix table 45, printed p.56]. Applying that price to the reported 1.5m eggs/month capacity gives **TRY 5.58m annual gross revenue**, assuming full output and every egg sold. This is a conditional illustration, not observed Çiftlik Bank sales. Feed, replacement birds, mortality, labor, electricity, packing, transport, veterinary costs, depreciation and taxes still have to be paid. A special blue-egg premium requires actual buyer invoices and volume; it cannot be imposed on all eggs.

**Dairy.** The National Milk Council's October–December 2017 reference price was TRY 1.40/litre [S27]. Its December-input cost model reports approximately TRY 1.18/litre at 20 litres/day, including depreciation and a calf/manure credit [S28]. The modeled difference is about **TRY 4.40 per cow per day**. With 250 continuously productive cows, the annualized model gives **TRY 401,500**, before any additional unmodeled central/distribution costs. This is a deliberately transparent scenario, not a claim that the observed 250-head capacity [S26] comprised 250 continuously lactating cows. Dry periods, herd mix, disease and lower utilization can reduce it; profitable operations can still fail to produce enough cash to meet member claims.

**Meat/livestock.** Animals require feeding and biological time before sale. Gross carcass receipts must be netted against purchased stock, feed, weight loss, mortality, veterinary costs, labor and capital charges. No verified cycle-level cost, slaughter volume or margin was obtained. Proposed herds do not fund current payouts.

**Retail/processing.** The difference between producer and consumer prices is not the central company's profit: procurement, shrinkage, wages, rent, refrigeration, transport, franchise economics, tax and credit losses intervene. Transfers of goods inside the group must cancel when measuring external revenue. Franchise opening fees and game-credit sales are separate from recurring food profit. No consolidated third-party retail margin statement was available. C51 records Aydın’s sales/profit assertion as counter-evidence, without accepting it as an audited group result or matching it to a different-period payout total.

**Mining.** Section 11 applies a separate output/energy/capex test. BTC appreciation and exchange turnover cannot be substituted for realized mining profit.

Conclusion: the primary regulatory evidence rejects economic sufficiency, and the transparent scale checks do not provide a plausible rescue of the claimed productive-funding narrative. These checks do not estimate an exact “realistic agriculture ROI” for an unobserved business. Actual invested productive capital and net cash margins are missing, so a precise attainable portfolio return cannot honestly be calculated.

## 16. Failure mechanism

The evidence supports this reconstruction, with the attribution boundary preserved:

1. Deposits financed software claims whose apparent growth encouraged reinvestment and recruitment [C05–C09].
2. Some receipts returned to participants, producing credible payment experiences [C11, C42].
3. The real-business explanation failed the demonstrated-profit/sufficiency test [C16, C18–C21].
4. Funds moved through companies, jurisdictions and personal accounts, making consolidated ownership and availability difficult to establish [C33–C35].
5. Obligations could keep accruing in the game while cash was tied up, transferred or unavailable. A displayed balance could grow without a matching external productive receipt.
6. Withdrawal interruption exposed the financing dependence; enforcement and foreign forfeiture further constrained any recovery [C35–C36, C44].

Steps 5–6 are analytical synthesis, not a claim to possess the missing bank ledger.             + (unrealized_PnL_net_end − unrealized_PnL_net_start)
            + approved_nonperformance_adjustments

R01 includes entry costs in cost basis and models exit costs in liquidation value. Fees/slippage must therefore **not be subtracted again** from already-net PnL. If a gross convention is used instead, costs and mark reserves need explicit single deductions. The user's general bridge is adopted with these necessary clarifications: external cashflow must not already include withdrawals if withdrawals are subtracted separately, and unrealized PnL must be a period change. Multi-currency extensions must separate FX valuation from cash transfers; none is implemented here.
