# RIE-006 — Institutional Edge Intelligence & Advanced Alpha Mining

Status: **PREREGISTERED RESEARCH TRACK / PUBLIC-LEGAL SOURCES ONLY / P10 UNTOUCHED / P11 LOCKED**

Date established: **2026-09-28**

## 1. Objective

Create a systematic intelligence track that studies how sophisticated market
participants discover, validate, execute, diversify, protect and scale economic
edge, then converts only legally obtained public information into explicit YATL
research hypotheses.

The objective is not to copy a named firm's proprietary system. The objective is
to infer reproducible economic mechanisms from public evidence and test them
independently inside YATL.

This track is an input to Research Intake Engine and Alpha Factory. It has no
trade, quantity, risk, execution, P10, P11 or Live authority.

## 2. Non-negotiable legal and safety boundary

Allowed source classes:
- public court and regulatory filings;
- public enforcement documents and public exhibits;
- academic papers and conference material;
- patents;
- exchange documentation and market-maker program documentation;
- public technical talks, interviews and engineering posts;
- public repositories and reproducible notebooks;
- public job descriptions when used only to infer technical capability classes;
- public market and on-chain datasets with timestamped provenance;
- publicly available benchmark and microstructure research.

Forbidden inputs or methods:
- stolen or leaked proprietary source code whose acquisition/use is unauthorized;
- credential theft, account compromise, intrusion, malware or exploit use;
- bypassing paywalls, access controls, rate limits or authentication;
- material non-public information;
- private customer/user information;
- manipulation methods such as spoofing, wash trading or deceptive volume
  creation as alpha-generation mechanisms;
- front-running based on confidential order information;
- evasion of exchange, legal or regulatory controls.

If a public legal filing describes prohibited behavior, YATL may study it as a
risk/failure pattern but must not operationalize it.

## 3. Institutional intelligence taxonomy

Every extracted mechanism must be classified into one or more of these lanes:

- **IEI-01 Predictive Alpha** — public evidence of price/return forecasting
  mechanisms.
- **IEI-02 Execution Alpha** — reducing implementation shortfall, adverse
  selection, spread/slippage or timing loss.
- **IEI-03 Liquidity & Capacity** — determining when an edge can absorb capital
  without destroying itself.
- **IEI-04 Portfolio & Risk Alpha** — allocation, hedging, exposure shaping and
  loss-containment mechanisms that improve net economic outcome.
- **IEI-05 Market Microstructure** — order-book, queue, imbalance, impact,
  inventory and short-horizon information-flow mechanisms, only when trustworthy
  data exist.
- **IEI-06 Relative / Lead-Lag** — cross-asset or cross-symbol information
  propagation using only admitted long-only Spot research data.
- **IEI-07 Time / Event Structure** — session, release-time, auction-like,
  recurring temporal or event-conditioned effects using point-in-time inputs.
- **IEI-08 Alternative Public Data** — timestamped public on-chain, network,
  issuance, flow, market-structure or other external context.
- **IEI-09 Edge Decay / Crowding** — evidence that profitability changes as a
  mechanism becomes crowded, copied or capacity-constrained.
- **IEI-10 Meta-Research** — methods for finding, falsifying, clustering and
  prioritizing edges rather than a trading rule itself.

A source name is not an alpha family. Multiple sources describing the same
economic mechanism map to one deduplicated hypothesis family.

## 4. Required extraction packet

No institutional claim enters Alpha Factory as a candidate until an extraction
packet records:

- exact public source identity and retrieval date;
- source class and provenance;
- what is factually documented versus inferred;
- economic mechanism;
- required data;
- decision timestamp semantics;
- signal equation/rules when reproducible;
- execution assumptions;
- holding horizon;
- expected opportunity frequency;
- cost sensitivity;
- likely capacity sensitivity;
- known legal/market-structure limitations;
- possible leakage and survivorship risks;
- duplication fingerprint versus current candidate registry;
- YATL adaptation notes;
- falsification test;
- status: SOURCE_BOUND / METHOD_SPECIFIED / BLOCKED_SOURCE /
  BLOCKED_DATA / REJECTED_DUPLICATE / READY_FOR_PREREGISTRATION.

Reported source performance is untrusted metadata only.

## 5. High-priority research programs

### IEI-P1 — Execution Alpha Laboratory

Hypothesis:
a meaningful fraction of achievable net PnL may come from execution quality
rather than better directional prediction.

Research dimensions:
- implementation shortfall;
- spread crossing versus delayed entry;
- adverse-selection proxies;
- fill probability;
- slippage as a function of volatility/liquidity;
- execution latency sensitivity;
- turnover interaction;
- capacity decay.

Current YATL path remains Paper/Research only. No order endpoint is added.

### IEI-P2 — Cross-Sectional / Relative Opportunity

Study whether the broad historical Spot universe contains reproducible
cross-sectional information that single-symbol BTC/ETH research cannot observe.

Examples:
- relative strength;
- leader/laggard relationships;
- breadth;
- dispersion;
- cross-sectional rebound;
- liquidity-adjusted ranking;
- market-leader information propagation.

All mechanisms must respect point-in-time symbol eligibility and no-short current
governance.

### IEI-P3 — Liquidity Dislocation & Recovery

Study temporary dislocations where price, volume and liquidity conditions
diverge.

Questions:
- which dislocations mean-revert versus continue?
- when does liquidity recovery precede price recovery?
- can a deterministic signal distinguish panic continuation from rebound?
- how sensitive is the edge to costs and participation rate?

### IEI-P4 — Regime-Conditional Edge Discovery

Instead of requiring every edge to work in all regimes, search for edges with a
preregistered operating envelope and reliable abstention outside it.

Requirements:
- detector defined before evaluation;
- no post-outcome relabeling;
- opportunity-starvation audit;
- explicit unknown-regime state;
- separate candidate ID from any failed all-regime predecessor.

### IEI-P5 — Public Alternative-Data Laboratory

Potential source families:
- timestamped on-chain activity;
- stablecoin issuance/flow;
- exchange inflow/outflow where historically reconstructable;
- public network activity;
- funding/liquidation information only as research context when permitted by the
  no-Futures execution boundary;
- public macro/event releases with release-time provenance.

No alternative dataset is admitted without a point-in-time leakage audit.

### IEI-P6 — Edge Half-Life & Crowding Intelligence

Measure whether a candidate's economics weaken over time or with rising
participation proxies.

Outputs:
- rolling after-cost expectancy;
- turnover/capacity interaction;
- structural-break diagnostics;
- crowding proxies;
- candidate half-life estimate with uncertainty;
- retirement or reduced-capacity recommendation for future research generations.

Historical decay analysis cannot silently retune an active Forward candidate.

### IEI-P7 — Small-Edge Ensemble Research

Large systems may be built from multiple modest independent effects rather than
one spectacular signal.

Rules:
- components must first survive standalone Development gates;
- independence is measured economically, not by indicator name;
- combination weights are preregistered or mechanically constrained;
- combined costs, turnover and common-failure regimes are measured;
- Fresh OOS is spent only after ensemble freeze.

### IEI-P8 — Adversarial AI Falsification

Use AI primarily as a hostile research critic.

For every serious survivor, ask AI-assisted audit processes to search for:
- hidden lookahead;
- timestamp leakage;
- survivorship bias;
- data revisions;
- accidental future universe knowledge;
- duplicate economic mechanisms;
- unstable parameter neighborhoods;
- cost underestimation;
- outlier dependence;
- regime concentration;
- implementation assumptions that cannot be realized;
- simpler baselines that explain the result.

AI may propose counterexamples and new tests. It may not weaken gates after
seeing outcomes and may not directly execute trades.

### IEI-P9 — Mechanism-First Candidate Compiler

Convert external research into a machine-readable mechanism description before
writing strategy code.

Desired pipeline:

`public source → provenance → mechanism → exact hypothesis → required data →
falsification rule → duplicate check → candidate schema → preregistration`

This reduces narrative drift between “interesting idea” and what YATL actually
tests.

### IEI-P10 — Synthetic Market & Failure Generator

Build research-only synthetic scenarios designed to destroy fragile hypotheses:
- spread expansion;
- sudden liquidity withdrawal;
- regime transition;
- stale/missing observations;
- gap-like moves;
- whipsaw;
- correlated multi-symbol shock;
- delayed information;
- capacity/impact stress.

Synthetic success never substitutes for historical/OOS evidence. Its role is to
expose brittleness cheaply before scarce evidence is spent.

## 8. Success metrics

This track is judged by:
- number of high-quality public sources reviewed;
- distinct mechanisms extracted;
- duplicate-rate;
- reproducible candidate count;
- new independent alpha-family coverage;
- Development survivor count;
- OOS survivor count;
- incremental independent-edge clusters;
- execution/capacity improvement attributable to validated mechanisms;
- negative evidence retained;
- zero unauthorized data/access incidents.

It is not judged by sensational source claims or the number of strategies
generated.

## 9. Wealth / scale principle

The economic target is to maximize the probability of discovering and safely
scaling real after-cost edge.

No fixed wealth outcome is promised. Very large dollar outcomes require all of:
- persistent edge;
- many independent opportunity sources;
- sufficient capacity;
- low implementation leakage;
- robust risk control;
- capital preservation;
- long-horizon compounding.

YATL therefore optimizes the machinery that can discover scalable edge rather
than assuming a “magic bot” exists.
